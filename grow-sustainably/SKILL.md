# Grow Sustainably

Help grow a business sustainably without running out of money or energy.

## Core Principle

**Profitability is a superpower.** It gives infinite runway, clarity, and control. Spend less than you make. When profitable, you can take your time and make the right decisions at your own pace.

## Don't Spend Money You Don't Have

**Profit = Revenue - Costs**

### Two Types of Costs

- **Variable Costs (COGS)**: Scale with each unit sold (payment processing, hosting, fraud prevention)
- **Fixed Costs**: Don't scale with revenue (domain, hosting, people). #1 fixed cost is always people.

### Cost-Cutting Rules

1. **Pay yourself as little as possible, at least to start.** Increase salary as the company can afford it.
2. **Hire software, not humans.** Use Pilot/Bench for accounting, Gusto for payroll, Zapier for automation.
3. **Don't get an office.** Remote is default. Office comes later as a reward, if you want.
4. **Don't move to Silicon Valley.** Lower costs = faster profitability.
5. **Outsource everything.** You + robots first. Then freelancers. Then employees.

## Growth Mindset

- Don't need to dominate, disrupt, or conquer
- Small businesses are rarely eaten by big fish — big fish eat big fish
- Company grows as quickly as customers determine
- Working more hours ≠ faster growth

## Fundraising (If You Must)

- Bootstrap first. Profitability gives leverage.
- Consider Regulation Crowdfunding — turn customers into investors.
- Alternatives to VC: Earnest Capital, Indie.vc, Tinyseed Fund.
- With VC: profitability means lower dilution and retained control.

## Avoiding Burnout

1. **Running out of money** — solved by profitability
2. **Running out of energy** — equally dangerous

### Co-founder Relationships
- Approach like a marriage. Discuss success, exit, growth speed.
- Use vesting. Have hard conversations early.

### Personal Sustainability
- Don't treat it as all-or-nothing
- Hire when it hurts — mature business for new people to fit into

## Output

For any business decision, evaluate:
1. Impact on profitability (revenue and cost implications)
2. Reversibility (avoid irreversible decisions)
3. Whether it's driven by customer needs or ego/vanity
4. Whether there's a cheaper/simpler alternative
5. The "default alive or default dead" test
